# question_framing.md — IFT vs StatFEM on real ShockSat (Question/Decision gate §5)

## The engineering decision being supported
Is **"physics in the prior with a learned trust temperature β" (IFT/PIFT)** a better, equal, or worse way to
build a *trustworthy* ShockSat digital twin than our **StatFEM** (discrepancy / modal-feature) approach, on
**real held-out data**? Concretely: when we move the model-trust knob from the **likelihood** (StatFEM M2's σ_d)
to the **prior** (IFT's β) on the **identical** field observable (log|H|), does the twin become more accurate,
better calibrated, or more robust?

## Objective / success metric
Held-out peak-|H| + band-GRMS **proper scores** (interval/Winkler, CRPS, log-predictive-density), **median over
≥25 identical channel splits**, with **calibration** (coverage, z-std) and **all-variable convergence (incl. β)**
as gates. Lower interval/CRPS and higher logpd = better; coverage ≈ 0.95 and z-std ≈ 1 = calibrated. Proper
scores reward sharpness AND calibration jointly, so a method cannot win by inflating bands (the wide-doesn't-win
guard, `score.wideband_demo`).

## What the analysis is allowed to choose
- The IFT posterior over (θ on the 4 identifiable groups, β, global gain c).
- The sampler escalation (NUTS → TMCMC) IF a documented multimodality fires.
- Nothing about the verdict: the verdict STRING is generated by `check.py` from the proper-score numbers.

## What remains human judgment
- Whether the QOI / observable / band is the right one (inherited from the StatFEM card; Andrew's call).
- Whether a ROBUSTNESS-WIN (converges where M2 diverged, calibrated-but-wide) is "good enough" for the program's
  goals — a scope judgment, not a metric.

## PRE-COMMITTED verdict rule (written BEFORE running; ALL outcomes valid — honest negatives count)
The verdict is one of the following, chosen by `check.py` from the numbers:
- **WIN:** IFT converges (incl. β) AND beats L5 on headline proper scores (interval/CRPS/logpd, median over
  splits) at equal-or-better calibration (coverage/z-std).
- **ROBUSTNESS-WIN:** IFT does NOT beat L5 but AVOIDS the M2 whole-FRF failure — it converges and widens
  gracefully (β shrinks) where M2 diverged ⇒ physics-in-prior is more robust than physics-in-likelihood on the
  same field observable. (Directly tests Bilionis's claim on real data.)
- **TIE:** IFT ≈ L5 within the split-to-split spread ⇒ competitive, not better here.
- **HONEST NEGATIVE:** IFT also hits the coarse-FEM wall — it does not converge (incl. β), OR β collapses and it
  reduces to a wide regression no better than L5+discrepancy ⇒ physics-in-prior does not rescue a coarse FEM
  either.

## What would make this the WRONG question (stated up front)
The StatFEM run established that the **binding constraint is the coarse FEM** (the model-form ceiling: σ_w2≈4.4%
modal-frequency error; amplitude mis-level ×2; calibrated-but-wide ×2.7). If the ceiling binds, then **no
inference construction — IFT included — can sharpen past it**; the comparison is then about
**robustness / calibration / honesty**, not sharpness. So a SHARPNESS WIN is a-priori unlikely, and the live,
honest question is whether physics-in-prior is *more robust and equally calibrated* than physics-in-likelihood on
the identical observable. We say this in the deck, not after the fact.

## Decision supported either way
- If WIN/ROBUSTNESS-WIN: prefer physics-in-prior (β thermometer) for the field-likelihood regime where M2
  diverges — a concrete recommendation for the robust-twin program.
- If TIE/HONEST-NEGATIVE: the modal-feature L5 twin remains the working recommendation, and the result documents
  that the coarse FEM — not the inference construction — is the lever, sharpening the program's central finding.
